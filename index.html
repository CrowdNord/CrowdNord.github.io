    Help topics

    Answers to common recovery questions

    Short, honest answers to the questions people ask most. Every link goes to the platform’s own official help pages. Use the search at the top of the page to find an answer fast.

    XMy X (Twitter) account was hacked

    If you can still sign in, change your password, check the email and phone on the account, and sign out unknown sessions and apps (Settings → Security and account access → Apps and sessions). If you are locked out, use X’s official hacked-account help. Never give your password or codes to anyone offering to “recover” it.

    Official pages: X: hacked-account help · X: password reset help

    XMy X account is locked or limited

    X sometimes locks or limits accounts and asks you to verify something, such as a phone number, an email or a captcha, or to reset your password. Sign in on the web at x.com and follow the on-screen steps exactly. The account usually stays locked until those steps are finished.

    Official pages: X: locked and limited accounts

    XMy X account was suspended. How do I appeal?

    Read the suspension notice when you sign in. It often says which rule X applied and whether there are steps you can take. If you believe it was a mistake, submit one honest appeal through X’s official form, explain what the account is used for, and wait for X’s email reply. Sending many appeals can slow things down, and nobody outside X can reverse a suspension for you.

    Official pages: X: suspended accounts · X: appeal form

    AppealsHow long do appeals take?

    It varies by platform and case. Replies can come within a day or take a few weeks. Check your email, including spam, for messages from the platform, reply in the same thread if they ask a question, and avoid sending repeat appeals. Nobody can speed up a platform’s review for a fee.

    InstagramMy Instagram account was hacked

    Go straight to Instagram’s official hacked-account page and follow the steps. Instagram may send a link or code to your email or phone, or ask for a video selfie to confirm it’s you. Only do this on Instagram’s own site or app, never through a link someone sends you.

    Official pages: instagram.com/hacked

    InstagramMy Instagram account was disabled or suspended

    Sign in to the Instagram app or website and read the notice. If an appeal or review option is shown, use it there and follow the steps. You can find Instagram’s official guidance in its Help Center. Be wary of anyone offering to “unban” an account for money.

    Official pages: Instagram Help Center

    FacebookMy Facebook account was hacked

    Use Facebook’s official hacked-account page to secure the account and get back in. Afterwards, review your login alerts, active sessions and connected apps in Meta’s Accounts Center.

    Official pages: facebook.com/hacked · Meta Accounts Center

    FacebookMy Facebook account was disabled or suspended

    Sign in and read the notice. If Facebook offers a way to disagree with the decision or request a review, use it there. Official guidance is in the Facebook Help Center. Nobody outside Meta can restore a disabled account for you.

    Official pages: Facebook Help Center

    GoogleI can’t get into my Google or Gmail account

    Start at Google’s official account recovery page and answer as best you can, ideally from a device and place where you normally sign in. If the account was hacked, also follow Google’s hacked-account guide.

    Official pages: Google account recovery · Google: recovery tips · Google: hacked-account guide

    AppleMy Apple Account (Apple ID) is locked

    Use Apple’s own pages only. Reset your password at iforgot.apple.com. If you can’t, and you have no trusted device, you can request account recovery, which can take several days. Apple explains what to do when an account is locked or disabled.

    Official pages: iforgot.apple.com · Apple: locked or disabled account · Apple: request account recovery

    Any platformA hacker changed my email or phone number

    Look in your inbox for the platform’s security email about the change. Many include a link to reverse it or secure the account. Then use that platform’s official recovery page from the links on this site. Do not reply to texts or DMs that claim to be from “support.”

    Any platformI lost my phone or my 2FA device

    Use backup codes if you saved them. Otherwise, follow the platform’s official recovery flow for lost two-factor devices. If the phone was lost or stolen, you can locate or lock it with the official tools.

    Official pages: X: two-factor authentication · Apple: two-factor authentication · Google Find My Device · Apple Find My

    Any platformWhat are backup codes and should I share them?

    Backup codes are one-time codes that let you sign in if you lose your two-factor device. Store them somewhere safe and never share them with anyone, including CrowdNord. Anyone who asks for your codes is trying to take your account.

    Official pages: Google: sign in with backup codes

    ScamsA “support” account DMed me about my account

    Platforms do not DM you to fix your account, and we never DM first either. Don’t click the link and don’t share codes. Block and report the account, then check your account’s status only through the platform’s own app or website.

    ScamsSomeone offered to “hack back” or recover my account for a fee

    This is a common scam. “Hackers for hire” and “recovery experts” in replies usually take the money and disappear, or take over more of your accounts. Nobody can guarantee a platform will restore an account. Use only the official recovery pages, and report the scam.

    Official pages: ReportFraud.ftc.gov (US)

    ScamsI lost crypto or money to a scam. Can someone recover it?

    Be very careful. People who lost money are often targeted again by “recovery” services that ask for an upfront fee. Report the loss to the authorities and to the exchange or bank involved. CrowdNord helps with account recovery guidance, not recovering lost funds.

    Official pages: ReportFraud.ftc.gov (US) · FBI IC3 (US)

    Any platformHow do I secure my account after I get it back?

    Change your password to a unique one, turn on two-factor authentication (an authenticator app is better than SMS), save your backup codes, sign out unknown sessions, remove apps you don’t recognize, and check that the recovery email and phone are yours.

    Official pages: Google Security Checkup · Apple: account security · Meta Accounts Center

    Protect yourself

    Scam warning signs

    Account-recovery scams are common. If you see any of the signs below, stop, block, and report the account. Then use only the official links on this page.

    • Someone asks for your password, 2FA codes, or backup codes

      Official support will not ask for these. We will not either. Anyone who does is a scammer.

    • Someone asks for remote access to your device

      Do not install remote-access apps or share your screen with anyone offering to “recover” your account.

    • A recovery link arrives in a DM or a reply

      Do not follow it. Bookmark the official pages listed above and open them yourself.

    • An account claiming to be CrowdNord messages you first

      We never DM first. If they message you first, it is not us. Start from this site and use only the channels listed in our Contact section.

    • Someone contacts you from an account not listed on this page

      Our only accounts are listed in the Contact section: WhatsApp +1 (513) 780-9655, @CrowdNord on Telegram and Instagram, and crowdnord@yahoo.com. Anyone else claiming to be CrowdNord is a scammer.

    • They ask for gift cards, or for payment from an account not listed here

      We will never ask for gift cards. Any fee is explained before work starts, in writing, and only through the official channels on this site.

    • Replies that say “DM this expert” or promise a sure restore

      That is a common scam. Block and report them. Nobody can guarantee a platform will restore an account.

    • A request for ID photos or login codes in a DM

      Do not send ID photos or login codes to anyone who DMs you. Submit official forms only on the platform’s own site, from a device you control.
